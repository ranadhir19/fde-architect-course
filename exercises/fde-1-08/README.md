# FDE 1.08 Exercise — Convert a chat-log mess into a decision log

**Topic:** The engagement operating system (Layer 2). **Part (d) of FDE 1.08.**
Attempt this before watching the tutorial half of the lesson.

## The task

Read `chat-log-mess.md`. It is the Cedarbrook Home Goods engagement
(the fictional Capstone 1 customer) as it actually exists on paper
at the end of week 2: a team chat thread, two email fragments, and
one set of meeting notes, none of them written to be a record.
Decisions were made in there — real ones, with money and dates
attached — alongside opinions, jokes, a decision that was later
reversed without anyone saying so, one promise made by someone
with no authority to make it, and one question the whole team
believes was settled that never was.

Produce two artefacts:

1. `DECISIONS.md` from `decision-log-template.md`: every decision
   the mess actually contains, each with its date, its decider,
   the alternatives that were on the table, and its status today.
   A reversed decision is two entries, not one edited entry — the
   log shows the reversal, because the person who joins in week 6
   will otherwise rebuild the thing week 2 abandoned.
2. `stakeholder-update.md` from `stakeholder-update-template.md`:
   the weekly update the sponsor (the head of e-commerce) should
   have received at the end of week 2, derived only from the log —
   what was decided, what changed, what is blocked, and the one
   decision she still owes. If it is not in the log, it does not
   go in the update; that discipline is the point of the exercise.

## Done means

- Every decision in the mess is in the log, and nothing is in the
  log that the mess does not support. Invented decisions score
  worse than missing ones: a log people cannot trust is worse
  than no log.
- The reversal is recorded as a reversal: the superseded entry
  stays, marked superseded, pointing at the entry that replaced
  it, with the reason the mess gives for the change.
- The unauthorised promise is logged as what it is — a statement
  made in chat, not a decision — with the person who can actually
  decide it named as the open owner.
- The never-decided question appears under "raised, not decided",
  not as a decision. The log's most valuable section is the one
  that stops the team acting on a settlement that never happened.
- Graded with `RUBRIC.md`. `SOLUTION.md` is the reference answer,
  released after the attempt window.

## The habit this exercise is really testing

- A decision is a commitment with a decider, a date, and a cost
  of reversal. Chat is where decisions hide; the log is where
  they become findable by someone who was not in the room.
- Write the entry when the decision is made, in the words the
  decider used, and let later entries supersede earlier ones.
  Editing history feels tidy and destroys the only thing the
  log is for.
