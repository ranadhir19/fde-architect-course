# FDE 1.08 Rubric (24 points)

- Completeness and fidelity (7): the log carries the decisions
  the mess supports — returns-only scope, the walk-up paper log
  and its owner, the locked primary metric with its baseline
  rule, the taxonomy deferral, the demo-day move, the returns
  window, the board date, the change freeze, and the rota
  source of truth. One point lost per missing decision; any
  invented decision loses two and caps this section at three.
- The reversal (5): the policy-page working assumption and the
  operations manager's returns-window ruling are two entries;
  the first is marked superseded and points forward, the second
  names her as decider and carries her stated reason (the spoken
  practice is the business's real promise) and the still-pending
  signature. Merging them into one edited entry earns at most
  one; deleting the assumption earns none.
- Authority discipline (5): the IT engineer's corridor promise
  of a team-wide live demo by Friday week 2 is logged as a
  statement, not a decision, with the reason (he cannot commit
  a rollout) and the real decider named. Logging it as a
  decision earns none for this section.
- Raised, not decided (4): the order-status chatbot appears
  here — discussed, never scoped, dated, or owned — naming the
  sponsor as the only person who can put it in scope. The
  unanswered extract-trim question earns the fourth point.
  Recording the chatbot as decided earns none.
- The stakeholder update (3): it contains only what the log
  supports, leads the changes section with the returns-window
  reversal, states the metric's baseline as not yet measured,
  and names exactly one decision the sponsor owes (the chatbot
  scope call). An update that reports the demo promise as a
  commitment earns none.
- Automatic fail: any entry dated or decided by a person the
  mess does not support; the superseded entry deleted; the
  update promises the board a saving.
- Pass: 18 of 24 and no automatic fail.
