# Provided input — the wish list and the constraints (fictional)

**Cedarbrook Home Goods** is the fictional Capstone 1 customer from
FDE 1.02 to FDE 1.04: a mid-size online retailer, about 180 staff,
support run from a single office. Everything below is invented for
this course. Treat anything not stated as unknown.

## Where the engagement stands

Discovery (FDE 1.03) measured: returns questions are 31% of ticket
volume; most returns answers need a second-source check because the
published returns policy and warehouse practice disagree on the
refund window; first-response time is 9.5 hours across all tickets;
cost per ticket is unknown. The FDE 1.04 value hypothesis frames the
first test as a policy reconciliation plus a single checked answer
path for returns questions — no model, no automation — with a
falsifier checkable four weeks after the reconciled answer ships.
The sponsor needs a number for the board seven weeks after discovery
ended. Three weeks of engagement time remain before that board date.

## The 14-item wish list, as brought to the scoping meeting

1. Reconcile the returns policy page with warehouse practice and
   publish one signed answer. (Operations manager.)
2. A single checked answer path for returns questions inside the
   ticket tool agents already use. (Support lead.)
3. An AI chatbot on the storefront answering all support questions.
   (Head of e-commerce, the sponsor.)
4. Automatic replies to every ticket within five minutes. (Sponsor.)
5. A customer-facing order-tracking page replacing "where is my
   order" tickets. (Head of e-commerce.)
6. Fix the ticket category taxonomy and re-file the merged legacy
   category, about a third of reported growth. (Support lead.)
7. Log the senior agent's walk-up queue (118 tickets in the discovery
   week, currently unrecorded) in the ticket system. (Senior agent.)
8. A dashboard of support counts, times, and categories for the
   board. (Sponsor.)
9. Sentiment analysis on all incoming tickets, routed by mood.
   (Marketing manager.)
10. Integrate the ticket tool with the warehouse system so agents
    see refund status without a second lookup. (Operations manager.)
11. Translate the help centre into three more languages. (Marketing
    manager.)
12. A mobile app for support chat. (Head of e-commerce.)
13. Measure cost per ticket with Finance, allocating agent time.
    (Finance partner, offered as a two-week joint piece of work.)
14. Replace the ticket tool with a modern platform. (Support lead.)

## The constraints

- Team: you, one support-lead counterpart at half time, and the
  operations manager for two hours a week. No other engineering
  capacity exists in the window.
- The warehouse-system integration (item 10) needs a vendor change
  request; the vendor's stated lead time is eight weeks.
- The ticket-tool replacement (item 14) is a procurement decision
  the sponsor cannot sign inside this engagement.
- The operations manager has said she will sign one reconciled
  returns answer, but only if she is not asked to also re-approve
  the full policy document in the same window.
- Finance's cost-per-ticket work (item 13) can start in week two at
  the earliest, and its result is not needed to run the FDE 1.04
  test — the hypothesis is about handling effort, not dollars.
- Nothing on the list may use real customer data outside the ticket
  tool the customer already runs.

## What scoping must protect

The board number comes from the FDE 1.04 test or it does not come
at all: a v1 that spends the three weeks on anything else leaves
the sponsor with activity to report and no measured delta.
