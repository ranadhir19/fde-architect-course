# FDE 1.03 Exercise — Write the discovery plan

**Topic:** Discovery: the first two weeks (Layer 1). **Part (d) of FDE 1.03.**
Attempt this before watching the tutorial half of the lesson.

## The task

Read `capstone1-brief.md` — the customer for Capstone 1, a fictional
mid-size online retailer whose support lead has asked to "automate
support". That ask is the stated problem. Your job in this exercise is
not to solve it; it is to plan the two weeks that find out whether it is
the real one.

Write `discovery-plan.md` from `discovery-plan-template.md`. The plan
must cover:

- A stakeholder map: who you will meet, in what order, and what only
  that person can tell you.
- A shadowing schedule: which work you will watch being done, for how
  long, and what you are counting while you watch.
- The discovery interviews: the questions you will ask, and which of
  them separate the stated problem from the real one.
- The evidence you will collect before any scoping conversation: what
  data, whose permission you need to see it, and what "measured" means
  for each number you plan to quote later.
- Your exit criteria for day 10: the findings that would let you frame
  a value hypothesis (FDE 1.04), and the findings that would make you
  recommend not proceeding.

## Done means

- Every stakeholder on the roster in the brief is either scheduled or
  explicitly deferred, with a reason.
- At least half a day of the plan is shadowing agents doing the work —
  discovery done only in meeting rooms scores zero on the rubric.
- No solution, tool, or architecture is proposed anywhere in the plan.
  Discovery that starts with an answer is sales, not discovery.
- Graded with `RUBRIC.md`. `SOLUTION.md` is the reference answer,
  released after the attempt window.
