# FDE 1.03 Reference solution

Released after the attempt window. One defensible plan:

**Order of people.** Frontline agents and the senior agent first
(days 1–3, while shadowing), then the support lead (day 4, once you
have seen the work she describes), the returns supervisor and
operations manager (day 5), IT engineer (day 6, with a concrete extract
request in hand), head of e-commerce last (day 9 — the sponsor hears
findings, not the original ask repeated back).

**Shadowing.** Two half-days on the email and web-form queues plus one
morning on phone-note write-ups. Count: tickets per agent-hour,
tickets re-categorised by the agent before answering, tickets walked
to the senior agent's desk, and tickets whose real subject is returns.
Those four counts test the stated problem directly: if re-categorised
and returns-driven tickets dominate, the problem is the taxonomy and
the policy, not raw volume.

**Disproving questions.** To the support lead: "If volume fell 20%
tomorrow, what would still be broken?" To agents: "Show me the last
ticket the category list could not describe." To the operations
manager: "When did the returns policy last change, and did ticket mix
move after it?" Each answer can kill the automation framing.

**Evidence.** Ticket export for the last 90 days (operations manager
sign-off, requested day 1 because it is the long pole); the support
lead's spreadsheet, audited against a hand-counted sample week rather
than trusted; returns volumes from the warehouse supervisor's own log.
"Measured" for the headline claim means: volume growth split by
category-as-filed and category-as-corrected, so the 40% figure is never
quoted unsplit.

**Day-10 exit.** Proceed to framing if one workflow shows a countable
baseline and an owner. Recommend not proceeding if the growth turns
out to be one seasonal spike already receding, or if no one with
budget will own a metric — say so in writing either way.
