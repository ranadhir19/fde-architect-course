# FDE 1.06 Reference solution

Released after the attempt window. One defensible plan:

## Milestones

- **Week 1 — the falsifier clock starts.** The reconciled returns
  draft is written in the first two days from the discovery record
  (the disagreement is already documented; drafting does not need
  the operations manager present), sent to her before she returns,
  and signed on her first day back. Evidence: the signed answer is
  published where agents work, and the four-week falsifier window
  is dated in writing from that day. Everything else in week one
  is subordinate to this milestone.
- **Week 2 — agents work from the checked path on live tickets.**
  The checked answer path is confirmed by the support lead while
  she is still available, booked into the Wednesday change window
  booked during week one, and used on live returns tickets for a
  full day. Evidence: a day of returns tickets answered from the
  path with no second-source check, counted. Walk-up logging starts
  the same week, timed on its first day against the senior agent's
  no-extra-time condition.
- **Week 3 — the measurement slice holds through peak.** The
  hand-corrected categories are applied to the falsifier sample
  weeks, the walk-up count is running, and the one-page board
  readout is drafted from counts that exist — effort removed, not
  dollars saved. Evidence: the readout's every number traces to a
  count named in the plan. Week three needs almost nothing from
  the support lead, by design: her rota was known at planning time.

## Demo cadence

- **End of week 1, to the sponsor and operations manager:** the
  signed answer, live. Decision forced: the falsifier window is
  accepted as the board number's source, in the room.
- **End of week 2, to the support team, not the sponsor:** the
  checked path on their own tickets. Decision forced: agents adopt
  it as the default, or name what is wrong with it while fixing it
  is still cheap.
- **End of week 3, to the sponsor:** the draft board readout.
  Decision forced: the board hears effort removed, honestly
  labelled — or the sponsor says now, not at the board, that only
  dollars will do, which reopens scope rather than relabelling it.

## Risk register (six of the defensible set)

1. **Signature slips past day one of the manager's return.**
   Owner: the FDE. Signal: no reply to the draft sent during her
   leave by midday on her return day. Response decided now: the
   sponsor escalates the same day; if the signature is not in hand
   by end of week one, the board readout is re-dated in writing
   that week — the slip is surfaced while it is a week, not hidden
   until it is a missed board.
2. **The board date is the earlier one.** Owner: the sponsor.
   Signal: any written board paper deadline. Response: the plan
   already protects the earliest reading — the falsifier clock
   starts week one — and the readout is drafted in week three
   from the first sample week if the earlier date is confirmed.
3. **The support lead's week-three rota removes her entirely.**
   Owner: the FDE. Signal: the rota published in week two.
   Response: nothing in week three requires her; her confirmation
   work was pulled into week two for exactly this reason.
4. **The Wednesday change window is missed.** Owner: the FDE.
   Signal: no booking confirmation by end of week one. Response:
   book week one's window for the draft path configuration and
   week two's at the same time; a missed window slips the path by
   exactly one week and the register, not hope, absorbs it by
   moving the measurement slice earlier.
5. **Walk-up logging costs the senior agent real time.** Owner:
   the senior agent, with the FDE timing it. Signal: the day-one
   timing in week two exceeds two minutes per ticket. Response:
   cut the log to the three fields the baseline needs; if it still
   costs time, the count reverts to a sampled tally and the hole
   in the baseline is stated in the readout.
6. **Peak volume swamps the falsifier sample.** Owner: the support
   lead. Signal: week-two volume above the baseline week by a
   third. Response: the falsifier compares handling effort per
   returns question, not totals, and the peak uplift is reported
   alongside — a per-question measure survives a volume change
   that a totals measure would not.

## Ambiguity log

- **Board date: "end of the month" versus "in five weeks".**
  Assumption: plan to the earlier date. Settler: the sponsor, in
  writing. Plan breaks when: the earlier date passes with the
  contradiction still unsettled — after that, no reading of the
  brief supports a promise.
- **The signature: promised for a draft that does not exist.**
  Assumption: a draft written from the discovery record is what
  she meant by "the returns thing". Settler: the operations
  manager, on the draft itself. Plan breaks when: she requires the
  full policy re-approval she already declined — that is the
  FDE 1.05 cannot-succeed condition, and the plan stops there.
- **Logging time: agreed, untimed.** Assumption: under two minutes
  per ticket. Settler: the week-two timing. Plan breaks when: the
  timing says otherwise and the reduced log still costs time.
- **Peak uplift: unknown.** Assumption: the falsifier is measured
  per question, so the plan does not need the uplift. Settler:
  nobody can settle it in advance; it is reported, not assumed
  away.
