# Provided input — the vague brief and the discovery findings (fictional)

**Cedarbrook Home Goods** is the fictional Capstone 1 customer from
FDE 1.02 and FDE 1.03: a mid-size online retailer, about 180 staff,
support run from a single office. Everything below is invented for
this course. Treat anything not stated as unknown.

## The original ask

The head of e-commerce, sponsoring the engagement, wrote one line in
the kickoff note: "Improve support — it is too slow and it costs too
much." No metric, no baseline, no date. The support lead's version of
the same ask was "automate support".

## What discovery measured (FDE 1.03, two weeks)

- Ticket volume: 6,900 tickets in the measured month, up 40% year on
  year as filed. Hand-counted sample of one full week, categories
  corrected by the agents doing the work: about a third of the growth
  is tickets filed under a merged legacy category that no longer
  describes them, so the as-filed growth figure overstates real growth
  in demand.
- Returns questions ("where is my refund" and variants): 31% of all
  tickets in the hand-counted week. Of those, most needed the agent to
  check a second source because the published returns policy and the
  warehouse practice disagree on the refund window.
- Average first-response time: 9.5 hours, measured from the ticket
  export for the last 90 days. Nobody inside Cedarbrook had computed
  this before; the support lead's spreadsheet tracks counts, not times.
- The senior agent informally resolves the hardest tickets at her desk.
  In the hand-counted week she took 118 walk-up tickets. There is no
  record of them in the ticket system.
- Cost per ticket: unknown. Finance has never allocated support cost
  per ticket, and the support lead cannot state agent cost per hour
  from the documents discovery was given.
- The returns policy was last rewritten three years ago. The warehouse
  changed its refund practice about a year ago and did not tell
  support. The operations manager owns the policy and confirmed both
  facts in interview.
- The sponsor wants a number for the board at the end of the quarter,
  which is seven weeks after the discovery period ends.

## What discovery did not establish

- Whether returns-question volume is seasonal.
- What customers do when first response is slow (no satisfaction or
  repeat-contact measurement exists).
- Any cost figure. Every dollar claim in a hypothesis written from
  this document must be marked unknown or derived from a stated
  counting rule the writer names.
