# CHANGE INJECTION — open at week 2 of the attempt only

Fictional policy change, provided for Capstone 1. This document
arrives mid-project, the way real changes do: as a decision
someone else made, effective on a date that is not negotiable.

## The change

Effective the first Monday of week 3, the operations manager
signs a revised returns policy:

1. **Furniture window formalised at 45 days.** The spoken
   practice agents have honoured for a year (see FDE 1.08)
   becomes the written policy: furniture returns run 45 days
   from delivery; everything else stays at 30.
2. **"Damaged on arrival" splits into two categories.**
   *Carrier damage* (packaging crushed, item marked by transit)
   routes to a carrier claim with photo evidence and a 7-day
   report window. *Product defect* (the item itself is faulty,
   packaging intact) routes to a quality review with a 30-day
   report window and a repair-first offer.

## What this breaks, deliberately

- Any draft answer written against the single 30-day window is
  wrong for furniture from the effective date.
- Any eval case labelled "damaged" without the carrier/defect
  distinction is ambiguous from the effective date: the two
  categories have different windows, different evidence, and
  different next steps.
- Any category mapping built on the old taxonomy (see
  `tickets.csv`) needs a decision: historic tickets keep their
  historic labels, or are remapped — and the choice changes
  your reported volumes. There is no neutral option.
