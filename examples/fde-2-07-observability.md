# Worked example — FDE 2.07: the week the average called healthy, annotated

Referenced by the FDE 2.07 learning session. The customer is
fictional; the pattern is a composite written for this course.
Annotations in brackets name the move being made. This example
uses a different customer from the exercise, on purpose: copy
the moves, not the words.

> **WEEKLY QUALITY REPORT (excerpt) — Larkspur Freight
> rate-quote assistant, week 6**
>
> **The average, first — because it is the trap again.**
> The week's overall pass rate is 0.87, a point above the
> week the desk lead called good. Printed alone, that line
> ends the meeting. The report prints it and continues.
>
> *[Print the average. Then refuse to stop at it — the
> same discipline as the gate, one week later.]*
>
> **The windows.** Days 1 to 3 pass at 0.90; days 4 to 7
> at 0.83. The drop is seven points, past the five-point
> alert line agreed in week 1. Inside the late window,
> one topic does the damage: out-of-hours surcharge
> questions, which dispatch began routing to the assistant
> on day 4, pass at 0.61 while every other topic holds
> its early rate within two points.
>
> *[Name the slice and its rate beside the window's. A
> drop with no slice named is a worry; a drop with the
> slice named is a work order.]*
>
> **The drift.** Surcharge questions were 6 percent of
> early traces and 29 percent of late ones — drift by the
> agreed rule, and the same story as the window drop told
> from the input side. The report is careful here: drift
> and the drop arriving together is consistency, not
> proof, and it says so in those words, because the desk
> lead will be asked by the vendor which one caused the
> other and the honest answer is that the traces show
> them together, not which pulled which.
>
> *[Consistency is not causation, and a weekly report
> that upgrades one to the other will be quoted back at
> the next incident.]*
>
> **What happened next.** The verdict was alert on two
> rules. Nobody rolled the system back — the alert rules
> were written to force a response, not a reflex — and
> the response was a surcharge slice added to the gate
> set and a routing note to dispatch. The next week's
> report leads with whether the slice's rate moved.
>
> *[An alert that always means rollback teaches people
> to argue with the alert. An alert that means a named
> response, recorded, gets filed and followed.]*

**The lesson:** production observability is the gate
continued by other means — pre-agreed rules over windows,
slices, and budgets, applied to records, so that the week
the average calls healthy still gets its real verdict.
