# Worked example — FDE 3.02: the artefact is the evidence, annotated

Referenced by the FDE 3.02 learning session. The customer is
fictional; the pattern is a composite written for this course.
Annotations in brackets name the move being made. This example
uses a different customer from the exercise, on purpose: copy
the moves, not the words.

> **EVIDENCE MAP (excerpt) — Larkspur Freight quote-desk
> build, SOC 2 landing review**
>
> **The control everyone believed in had no artefact.**
> Access reviews were "done quarterly" — the operations
> lead said so, the team believed it, and the last signed
> review in the file was eleven months old. The control
> had not failed; the evidence had simply stopped being
> produced when the person who ran it changed roles. The
> map scored it stale, and the landing conversation
> changed in one sentence: not "are reviews done?" but
> "who signs the next one, and when?"
>
> *[A control is its evidence, not its intention. Stale
> evidence usually marks an owner who left, not a team
> that stopped caring — which is why the map scores
> ownership as a separate check.]*
>
> **The missing report that was good news.** The quote
> desk's retention control named a monthly enforcement
> report that had never been run. Running it — an hour's
> work — showed retention was enforced everywhere except
> one legacy export bucket nobody remembered creating.
> The gap the map recorded as missing became, on closing,
> the only real finding of the review.
>
> *[Producing the artefact is not paperwork about the
> control; it is the first actual test of it. A missing
> report is unscored risk, not administrative delay.]*
>
> **The gate was named before the counts.** The map
> opened with its one gate gap — session logging on the
> pricing path, evidence present but unowned since the
> platform lead's departure — and only then with its
> eleven satisfied controls. The sponsor later said the
> ordering was why she trusted the rest of the page: a
> map willing to lead with its own stop sign is a map
> whose green lights mean something.
>
> *[Gate first is not pessimism; it is what makes the
> satisfied counts elsewhere in the map worth reading.]*

**The lesson:** in a regulated deployment, audit evidence
is a build artefact like any other — produced on a
schedule, owned by a name, and checked for freshness —
and the evidence map is the deliverable that turns "we
do that" into something an auditor, a compliance lead,
or a customer can verify without trusting you.
