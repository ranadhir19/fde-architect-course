# Worked example — FDE 1.04: framing a value hypothesis, annotated

Referenced by the FDE 1.04 learning session. The customer is fictional;
the framing is a composite written for this course. Annotations in
brackets name the move being made. This example uses a different
customer from the exercise, on purpose: copy the moves, not the words.

> **The ask, as received:** "Our dispatchers are drowning. Get us an
> AI dispatcher." — operations director, fictional regional courier
> firm, Larkspur Freight.
>
> *[An ask that names a tool is a conclusion, not a problem. The
> framing step starts by refusing the tool while accepting the pain:
> "drowning" is real, "AI dispatcher" is a guess about its cause.]*
>
> **What a week of shadowing showed:** dispatchers spend the first 90
> minutes of every shift rebuilding the day's route board by hand,
> because the overnight system orders stops by postcode while drivers
> actually run them by depot gate time. Everything after that —
> exceptions, late pickups, customer calls — fits in the remaining
> hours without overtime in four of the five observed days.
>
> *[The stated problem was total workload. The observed problem is
> one 90-minute workflow at the start of the shift. Framing shrinks
> the problem until it has a baseline.]*
>
> **The hypothesis:** If the route board is generated in gate-time
> order, dispatcher time on board-building falls from a measured 90
> minutes per shift to under 30 minutes within two weeks, freeing the
> hour for exception handling. Falsifier: if board-building time is
> unchanged in a timed sample in week two, the ordering was not the
> cost — the re-keying between systems was — and we stop and re-frame
> around data entry instead.
>
> *[Every element of the template is present in four sentences:
> workflow, measured baseline, intervention, predicted delta with a
> date, and a falsifier that names the rival explanation. Note also
> what the hypothesis does not claim: no headcount saving, no dollar
> figure, because neither was measured.]*
>
> **The lesson:** a value hypothesis is a bet you have agreed how to
> lose. If a framing cannot say what result would prove it wrong, it
> is not a hypothesis — it is a sales deck with the prices removed.
