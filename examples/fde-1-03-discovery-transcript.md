# Worked example — FDE 1.03: a discovery interview, annotated

Referenced by the FDE 1.03 learning session. The customer is fictional;
the interview is a composite written for this course. Annotations in
brackets name the move being made.

> **Manager:** "We need the chatbot to deflect more tickets. Deflection
> is at nine percent and the vendor promised thirty."
>
> *[The stated problem arrives with a vendor's number attached. The
> FDE does not argue with it — and does not accept it as the frame.]*
>
> **FDE:** "Before we talk about the bot — walk me through yesterday.
> What did the team actually spend its hours on?"
>
> *[Open question about real work, not about the tool. Discovery asks
> about the day, not the roadmap.]*
>
> **Manager:** "Honestly? Returns. Half the morning is 'where is my
> refund', and the policy page says one thing while the warehouse does
> another, so agents have to check both."
>
> **FDE:** "How would we count that — how many tickets yesterday were
> returns questions, and how many of those needed the warehouse check?"
>
> *[The moment a claim appears, ask how it would be counted. A claim
> nobody can count is a hypothesis, and gets written down as one.]*
>
> **Manager:** "Nobody counts it. The category just says 'general'."
>
> **FDE:** "Then that is my first finding, and it is free: the
> category system cannot see your biggest workflow. Let me shadow the
> morning queue tomorrow and hand-count it, and we will know by Friday
> whether this is a deflection problem or a returns-policy problem
> wearing a deflection costume."
>
> *[The stated problem ("raise deflection") and the candidate real
> problem ("returns policy plus an invisible category") are now both
> on the table, with a cheap test and a date. That is the whole move
> FDE 1.03 teaches: never leave an interview with only the problem you
> were handed.]*
