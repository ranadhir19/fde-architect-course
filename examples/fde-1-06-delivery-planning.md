# Worked example — FDE 1.06: a plan that survives contact, annotated

Referenced by the FDE 1.06 learning session. The customer is fictional;
the planning is a composite written for this course. Annotations in
brackets name the move being made. This example uses a different
customer from the exercise, on purpose: copy the moves, not the words.

> **The room:** Larkspur Freight (from the FDE 1.04 and 1.05
> examples). The scoped v1 is two items: generate the morning route
> board in depot gate-time order, and time the board-building work
> for two weeks so the value hypothesis has its falsifier data. The
> brief says the depot manager wants it "before peak", gives no
> date for peak, and the one clerk who builds the board by hand is
> retiring — on a date given as "end of next month" in one message
> and "in three weeks" in another.
>
> *[The plan's first job is to find the date the whole plan hangs
> on and refuse to let it stay vague. Here that date is the
> retirement, not the peak: the board must be generated while the
> clerk is still there to say whether its order is right.]*
>
> **The milestone that matters:** by the end of week two, the
> generated board is the board the depot actually uses for one full
> morning, with the clerk present to catch its errors. Evidence is
> the morning itself, not a finished generator.
>
> *[A milestone is a morning the customer lives through, not a
> component completing. If the generated board is wrong, the plan
> learns it in week two with the one expert still in the building —
> which is the entire reason the sequence is what it is.]*
>
> **The risk register's first line** names the retirement-date
> contradiction, owned by the depot manager, with the response
> decided on day one: plan to the earlier date, and if the later
> date is confirmed, the spare week goes to timing data, not to
> new scope.
>
> *[An ambiguity with a decided response is a planning asset. The
> contradiction did not go away; the plan stopped depending on
> which version was true.]*
>
> **The lesson:** a delivery plan is not a prediction. It is a
> sequence arranged so the plan learns what it most needs to know
> while the people who know it are still reachable — and a register
> that says, in advance, what happens when the world disagrees.
