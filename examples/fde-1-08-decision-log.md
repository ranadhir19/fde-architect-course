# Worked example — FDE 1.08: a decision log that earns its keep, annotated

Referenced by the FDE 1.08 learning session. The customer is
fictional; the log is a composite written for this course.
Annotations in brackets name the move being made. This example
uses a different customer from the exercise, on purpose: copy
the moves, not the words.

> **DECISIONS.md — Larkspur Freight route-board engagement**
> (from the FDE 1.04 to 1.07 examples)
>
> ### D-01 — We instrument before we automate — Mon wk1
> - **Decider:** depot manager.
> - **Decided:** two weeks of route-board timing data are
>   collected before any automation is proposed.
> - **Alternatives considered:** automating the board build
>   immediately (the original ask).
> - **Why:** "We do not know which mornings are slow, and I
>   will not fund a fix for a morning we cannot describe."
> - **Status:** ACTIVE.
> - **Cost of reversal:** the baseline is unrecoverable once
>   the process changes; this decision cannot be remade later.
>
> *[The entry is written the day the decision is made, in the
> decider's own reason. Note what it protects: not the plan —
> the evidence. A baseline decision has an infinite cost of
> reversal, and the log is the only place that fact survives
> the week it was said in.]*
>
> ### D-02 — The morning board shows yesterday's jobs only — Tue wk2
> - **Decider:** FDE, with the depot manager's agreement in the
>   planning meeting.
> - **Decided:** jobs arriving after the 18:00 cut-off appear
>   on the next board, not the morning board.
> - **Status:** SUPERSEDED by D-04.
>
> *[D-02 is not edited when D-04 lands. It is marked, and it
> stays — because a driver was briefed against D-02 for three
> weeks, and when he asks why the board changed, the answer
> is an entry, not an apology.]*
>
> ### D-04 — Cut-off moves to 20:00; late jobs join the morning board — Thu wk4
> - **Decider:** depot manager.
> - **Decided:** the cut-off moves to 20:00 after the partner
>   depot's file reliably lands by 19:30; late jobs are
>   flagged on the board, not held.
> - **Why:** the 18:00 rule was protecting against a file that
>   no longer arrives late.
> - **Status:** ACTIVE. Supersedes D-02.
>
> *[The reversal names what changed in the world, not who was
> wrong. D-02 was right for the file as it was; D-04 is right
> for the file as it is. A log that makes reversals look like
> errors teaches the team to stop recording them.]*
>
> **The lesson:** the log is not minutes and it is not a diary.
> It is the engagement's memory of its commitments — who made
> them, what they cost to undo, and which ones the world has
> already replaced. Keep it at decision grain, keep it the
> day it happens, and never rewrite it.
